About me

I am a PhD student in Computer Science and Engineering at the Hong Kong University of Science and Technology. Previously, I received my bachelor’s degree from the University of Science and Technology of China.

My research focuses on efficient training and inference for graph neural networks and large language models. I work on optimization, knowledge distillation, model compression and GPU kernels, with the goal of making powerful models practical to train and deploy.

Research interests: Graph learning · LLM compression · ML systems

I welcome discussions about efficient ML and research opportunities. Feel free to email me!

Publications

PACE-GNN: Cost-Aware Progressive Graph Granularity Scheduling for Faster Convergence in GNN Training

Qiqi Zhou, Yanyan Shen, Lei Chen

ICDE 2027Accepted

Start with an inexpensive community graph, then selectively recover finer detail as training progresses. A cost-aware schedule and asynchronous graph refinement accelerate convergence while preserving final quality.

Up to 3.60× faster than full-graph training; 6.35× faster than neighbor sampling.

Faster Convergence in Mini-batch Graph Neural Networks Training with Pseudo Full Neighborhood Compensation

Qiqi Zhou, Yanyan Shen, Lei Chen

VLDB 2025

Neighbor sampling loses both messages and gradient contributions. PFNC uses compact historical embedding and gradient caches to compensate for both sources of error, improving convergence across sampling strategies.

Evaluated across 7 graph benchmarks and 8 sampling methods.

Narrow the Input Mismatch in Deep Graph Neural Network Distillation

Qiqi Zhou, Yanyan Shen, Lei Chen

KDD 2023

A shallow student and a deep teacher see different graph neighborhoods. The Bayesian Receptive Field Expander estimates missing information at the graph and node levels to make knowledge transfer more effective.

Ongoing research

AdaSSD: Adaptive Subspace-Shared Delta Compression for Multiple LLMs

Qiqi Zhou, Yanyan Shen, Lei Chen

Under reviewLLM compression

Discover shared subspaces across specialized LLMs instead of anchoring every model to a fixed pretrained reference. Shared bases, private corrections and low-bit residuals support compressed-format inference and faster model switching.

63.3–79.3% smaller switch payloads and 1.62–2.08× faster switching for three 7B specialists.

Work in progressCUDA · Graph learning

ROF: A Reuse-Oriented Framework for Accelerating Graph Neural Networks Training

Co-design graph reuse structures and CUDA aggregation kernels to avoid redundant computation and memory access. Experiments show up to 1.61× faster training than a standard PyTorch Geometric implementation.
